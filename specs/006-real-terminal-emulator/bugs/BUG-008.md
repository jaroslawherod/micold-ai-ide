# Bug Report: a single left click in a terminal pane selects the clicked cell — it stays highlighted, and it overwrites the clipboard with that one character

**Type**: Implementation defect (a zero-length press is turned into a one-cell selection; no
requirement said a click without a drag selects nothing, so nothing tested it)
**Severity**: Medium (the highlight is cosmetic, but a click over text silently replaces whatever the
user had on the clipboard with a single character — the same unrecoverable loss FR-013c exists to
prevent, reached through a different gesture)
**Feature**: `006-real-terminal-emulator` (FR-013, FR-013a, FR-013c; US2). First noticed during the
BUG-004/005/006 visual pass (`evidence/bugfix-pass-2026-09-13.md`, "Observed, not in scope");
reproduced on `origin/main` at `226d3a8b`
**Reported**: 2026-09-14
**Status**: **Fixed**. A `Char` selection is empty until its first update. The pane sends no update while the
pointer stays in the pressed cell. The left release asks the shell to copy its current selection
(`TerminalSelectionReleased`), so a click highlights nothing and copies nothing.
**Patched**: 2026-09-14. FR-013e and SC-015 added; tasks T081–T083 (Phase 15).
**Fixed**: 2026-09-14, in Phase 15 (T081–T083).
- Gated by `selection::tests` (`a_click_without_a_drag_selects_nothing`,
  `an_update_onto_the_start_anchor_selects_that_cell`, `a_drag_out_and_back_selects_the_pressed_cell`).
- Gated by `tests/clipboard_request.rs::a_click_without_a_drag_asks_for_nothing`.
- Gated by `terminal_pane::tests::clipboard_gestures`.
- Visual pass: [evidence/bugfix-008-pass-2026-09-14.md](../evidence/bugfix-008-pass-2026-09-14.md).

## Description

In a terminal pane with no mouse-reporting program running (or with Shift held under one), press and
release the left button over a cell without moving the pointer to another cell:

1. The clicked cell is drawn with its colours swapped — the selection highlight — and stays that way
   until the next press anywhere in the pane.
2. If the cell holds a character, releasing the button auto-copies that one character to the
   clipboard, replacing what was there.

When the cell is blank (past the end of the line's text) the copied text trims to empty and the
FR-013c guard leaves the clipboard alone, which is why the 2026-09-13 pass saw only the highlight.

**Expected**: a click is not a drag. It clears any existing selection and selects nothing: no cell is
highlighted and the clipboard is untouched. Dragging to another cell selects as before, and
double- and triple-click still select a word and a line.

## Reproduction

**Unit** (added by T081; fails against `origin/main`'s code):
`selection::tests::a_click_without_a_drag_selects_nothing` — `Selection::start(anchor, Char, …)`
with no update reports `contains(anchor) == true` (and `text()` returns the clicked character).

**End to end** — Xvfb `:94` + lavapipe, 2026-09-14, client and daemon built together from
`origin/main` `226d3a8b` and pinned to a private directory; private `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`, throwaway
project seeded through `projects.json`:

1. Open a Regular Terminal instance, run `PS1='$ '; clear; echo hello world`.
2. Click once on the `w` of `world`, move the pointer away.
   **Actual**: `w` is drawn highlighted and stays so.
3. Drag across `hello`, then press **Ctrl+Shift+V**: `hello` is pasted (the clipboard works).
4. **Ctrl+U**, then click once on `w` at a human pace (button held ~0.4 s, pointer still), then press
   **Ctrl+Shift+V**.
   **Actual**: `w` is pasted — the click replaced `hello` on the clipboard. **Expected**: `hello`.

(A synthetic `xdotool click`, whose press and release land in one event batch, shows the highlight
but not the copy: the release is handled against the frame built before the selection existed. A
real click is slower than one batch, which is the step-4 path. The same stale read has a second
face: with a selection already held, a press and release delivered together — a touchpad tap, a
synthetic click — copy the **previous** selection over the clipboard, even though the click itself
selected nothing.)

## Root Cause Analysis

The press path treats every left press as the start of a character selection, and the selection
model has no notion of an empty one.

- `crates/micold-client/src/ui/material/terminal_pane.rs`, left `ButtonPressed` arm: a locally
  handled press sets `state.dragging = true` and publishes `TerminalSelectStart { kind: Simple }`.
- `crates/micold-client/src/main.rs`, `TerminalSelectStart`: builds
  `Selection::start(anchor, SelectGranularity::Char, …)` and stores it in `app.selection`.
- `crates/micold-client/src/selection.rs`, `Selection::start`: bounds are `expand(anchor, anchor,
  Char)` = `(anchor, anchor)`, and both `contains` and `text` treat the bounds as **inclusive** on both
  ends. A selection whose ends coincide therefore covers exactly the clicked cell — `contains` is true
  there (the pane's `draw` swaps that cell's colours) and `text` returns its character.
- `terminal_pane.rs`, left `ButtonReleased` arm (`state.dragging`): auto-copies
  `selectable_content()` when it is non-empty (FR-013c's guard), so a one-character "selection" is
  written.
- Nothing clears the selection on release; the next press replaces it, hence "until the next click".
- That release arm reads `selectable_content()` from the `selection` the pane was **built** with.
  iced 0.14 runs every event since the last redraw through one `user_interface.update` call
  (`iced_winit` `lib.rs`), and the app applies the published messages only afterwards. When a press
  and its release share a batch, the release therefore sees the selection from before the press and
  copies it — so fixing `Selection` alone would still let a tap overwrite the clipboard.

Inclusive ends are right for a drag (dragging from `h` to `o` must select both) and for `Word`/`Line`
granularity (a double-click with no motion must select the word). What is missing is the distinction
between a character selection whose moving end has never left the pressed cell — a click — and one
that has. Alacritty, whose model the pane follows, draws the same line: its selection is empty while
both ends sit on the same point, so a press with no motion selects nothing.

Why no test caught it: `selection.rs`'s tests always `update` a `Char` selection before asserting,
`clipboard_request.rs` builds selections the same way, and quickstart §10f checks single-click **and
drag**, never a click alone.

## Recommended Fix

Three parts, each closing one way a click reaches the highlight or the clipboard.

1. **The pane decides what a drag is, in screen cells.** Remember the pressed viewport cell in
   `PaneState`. While the pointer stays in that cell, `CursorMoved` publishes nothing. From the first
   move into another cell onwards, every move publishes `TerminalSelectUpdate`, including moves back
   onto the pressed cell. The pane has to decide this because it sees the pointer. An anchor is a
   `LineId`, and at the live bottom `row_line_id` moves forward with every line of output. So under
   streaming output, a same-cell jitter would reach `Selection` as an update onto a *different*
   anchor.
2. **`Selection` has an empty state.** A `Char` selection is empty until its first `update`,
   whatever that update's anchor is. The pane is the only place that decides what counts as a drag
   (part 1). Comparing `LineId` anchors here would decide a second time in different units: under
   streaming output, a real move to another row can land back on the start anchor. Until the first
   update, `contains` is false everywhere and `text` is empty. That
   leaves nothing to draw, and through the existing FR-013c guard nothing to copy. `Word` and `Line`
   selections are non-empty from the press, as now. A click still replaces, and so clears, any
   previous selection.
3. **The release copies the current selection, not the pane's view.** The pane's left-release arm
   stops writing `selectable_content()`. Instead it publishes a new
   `SessionMsg::TerminalSelectionReleased`. The shell resolves that with `selection::copy_request` on
   `app.selection`, after the batch's `TerminalSelectStart` and `TerminalSelectUpdate` have been
   applied in order. The copy then always matches the gesture that just ended, and a fast double-tap
   copies the word. It is a separate message from `TerminalCopyRequested`, whose handler also closes
   the terminal context menu. Releasing a drag should not do that.
