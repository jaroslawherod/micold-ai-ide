## Step 2 — focus gate (SC-003)

The **gating** works, exactly. With focus released by Ctrl+Shift+E, typing `UNFOCUSED-KEYS` put
nothing on the prompt; a click on the pane re-acquired focus and `FOCUSED-KEYS` appeared
immediately, character by character (no line buffering). US3's AS1, AS3 and AS4 all hold.

The **indicator** does not exist. `compare -metric AE` between the unfocused and focused frames is
`0 (0)` — full frame and cropped to the terminal area alone. Two states, different behaviour,
identical pixels. → **[BUG-005](../bugs/BUG-005.md)**.

## Step 3 — interactive `claude` (SC-001)

`claude` (Claude Code v2.1.245) launched inside the pane and rendered its full-screen UI correctly.

- **Arrow keys** — typing `/` opened the slash-command menu with the first entry highlighted; two
  `Down` presses moved the highlight to the third entry. `step3-slash-menu-arrows.png`.
- **Tab autocomplete** — typing `mode` filtered the menu to `/model`, `/code-review`,
  `/claude-code-setup:…`; `Tab` accepted the highlighted entry and the input became `/model [model]`.
  `step3-tab-complete.png`.
- **Esc** (step 4's companion clause) — reached `claude` and closed the menu. It did not close an
  app overlay.
- **Multi-line** — `Shift+Return` **submitted** rather than inserting a newline. This is *not* a
  defect of this terminal: `claude` needs `/terminal-setup` to install a distinguishable
  Shift+Enter sequence, and without it a standalone terminal sends a plain CR too. The documented
  multi-line entry (`\` + Enter, Alt+Enter) was not exercised.
- **Ctrl+C** — reached the process and cleared the input. The clause says "interrupt a *running*
  turn"; the turn had already completed when the chord landed, so **the interrupt-mid-turn path was
  not observed**. The same key path was proven end-to-end on the shell side instead: `sleep 300`
  interrupted with Ctrl+C echoed `^C`, returned the prompt, and the next command ran.

## Step 5 — copy / paste (FR-013)

Every gesture moves text correctly:

- drag-select → **auto-copy on release**, then `Ctrl+Shift+V` reproduced the selection exactly;
- `Ctrl+Shift+C` **with a selection** → correct;
- **middle-click** paste → `MID>>alpha beta gamma delta<<END`;
- **right-click menu** → a Copy/Paste menu appears, and both work.

Two defects on this surface:

1. `Ctrl+Shift+C` with **no** selection writes an empty string, destroying the clipboard's previous
   contents. The other two copy surfaces guard the empty case; the chord does not. →
   **[BUG-004](../bugs/BUG-004.md)**, `bug-004-clipboard-before.png` / `-after.png`.
2. **Paste is not bracketed.** Pasting two lines ran the first and left the second on the prompt.
   The quickstart's step 5 names this expectation explicitly. →
   **[BUG-006](../bugs/BUG-006.md)**, `bug-006-multiline-paste.png`.

## Step 6 — sizing, resize, reflow, scrollback (SC-004)

Both halves. The window was resized smaller and larger (`xdotool windowsize`) and the terminal
reflowed to the new column count each time, with no leftover artifacts. The sidebar's own resize
handle was dragged, narrowing and widening the pane — the same reflow, from the other direction.
Flooding the pane and scrolling with the wheel brought earlier output back.

## Step 7 — input discarded while not Running

Exiting the shell left the status bar reading **"exited"** beside a **"restart"** control
(`step7-not-running.png`). Keys pressed while not Running were **discarded, not buffered** — after
restart, none of them appeared. Scrolling, selection and copy all still worked while the session was
not Running, and a paste after restart landed correctly.

## Step 8 — responsiveness under flood (SC-008) — out of reach

SC-008 claims "≤~100 ms perceived" on a user's machine. lavapipe is a software rasteriser; its frame
pacing says nothing about a real GPU's. Measuring it here would produce a number that means nothing,
so it is reported unrun rather than passed.

## Step 9 — configurable scrollback (SC-007) — all three clauses

- **Validation** — `0` was rejected with "Enter a number between 100 and 1000000." and not written
  (2026-08-20, `step9-validation.png`).
- **A session started after the change honours the new limit** — see step 10g below: with the limit
  at 170, a session created afterwards retained exactly 170 rows.
- **The value persists across a relaunch** — the limit was set to 170, then client and daemon were
  stopped and relaunched. Settings reopened reading **170**.

That relaunch surfaced a separate defect: the app came back as **"Theme: Auto"** (dark) although the
user had chosen **Light** before quitting. The daemon persists a boot-time snapshot of the whole
settings struct — including the client-owned `theme` — whenever a service-owned field is saved. →
**[010 BUG-014](../../010-daemon-session-persistence/bugs/BUG-014.md)**.

## Step 10 — the bugfix checks

### 10a — touchpad scrolling (items 1–5)

Items 1–2 were confirmed by the user on Wayland/GNOME (recorded in T042's own note). Item 5 — the
scrollbar parks cleanly and hides at the live bottom — **passes** here. Items 3–4 ask about
**sub-line touchpad flicks**; `xdotool` emits only discrete wheel notches, so those are **out of
reach** by the same limit that puts item 13 out of reach.

### 10b — the scrollbar (items 6–10)

The scrollbar was located numerically rather than by eye — a PIL column scan puts the track at
x 1590–1598 and the thumb at x 1592–1597.

- **6, 7** — shows while scrolled back, hides at the live bottom. Re-confirmed.
- **8** — dragging the thumb follows the grab point exactly, with no drift over a full-track drag
  (`step10b-thumb-drag.png`).
- **9** — clicking the track above/below the thumb pages exactly one viewport each way
  (`step10b-track-paging.png`).
- **10** — the colours follow the theme *by construction*, not by a hardcode: the track is
  `Color { a: 0.08, ..fg }` and the thumb `Color { a: 0.5, ..fg }` over the palette's own
  foreground (`terminal_pane.rs:692-716`), so both track the scheme in either direction.

### 10c / 10d — mouse-mode gating and wheel granularity (items 11–15)

With `htop` running, wheel events are forwarded to the process (it scrolls its own list) rather than
moving the viewport; Shift forces the local behaviour. Leaving `htop` restores viewport scrolling
over the shell's own history. Item 13 (sub-line flicks) is out of reach; item 15's discrete-wheel
path is what `xdotool` produces natively and was exercised both over the pane and inside `htop`.

### 10e — focus lifecycle (items 16–19)

- **16** — a session started with the "+" control accepted typing **with no click at all**:
  `AUTOFOCUS-PROBE` appeared in `claude`'s input box (`step10e-autofocus-on-start.png`).
- **17** — verified by the user 2026-07-20, and re-observed here: clicking a session in the sidebar
  and typing without touching the pane reached that session's shell.
- **18** — Ctrl+Shift+E releases; the session stays "running".
- **19** — closing the displayed session cleared focus. `ZZZFOCUSCLEARED` typed afterwards reached
  no terminal; the remaining session's pane was unchanged when selected.

### 10f — selection granularity (items 20–23)

Single-click drag gives character-level selection; **double-click** selects exactly the word under
the pointer (`step10f-double-click-word.png`); **triple-click** selects the whole line; **Shift+drag
inside `htop`** selects despite mouse mode (FR-013b), and the auto-copy round-tripped the exact
three-line selection into a file (`step10f-shift-drag-htop.png`).

### 10g — the scrollback cap (item 24)

With the limit at **170**, a session created afterwards was flooded with 600 lines
(`for n in $(seq 1 600); do echo "CAP $n"; done`) and scrolled to the top.

The retained history is exactly 170 rows: 3 lines of pre-flood output, the 66 blank screen rows that
scrolled off behind them, and 99 `CAP` lines above the ~70 on screen. The oldest surviving flood line
is **`CAP 432`** — and 600 − 432 + 1 = 169, the flood's share of the cap
(`step10g-scrollback-cap.png`). Scrolling stops there; it does not misbehave.

Worth recording because the first reading looked like a failure: the top of the scrollback shows
pre-flood text and a band of blanks, which reads as "the cap was not applied". It is the opposite —
those are the oldest *retained* rows, and blank rows scrolled off a partly-empty screen are ordinary
terminal history. The scrollbar geometry said so independently before the line numbers did: solving
`thumb = track × screen / (history + screen)` on that frame gives history ≈ 172.
