# Bug Report: scrolling back through history fetches from the revealed line to the *live tail* every notch, so the pane goes blank for seconds

**Type**: Requirement not implemented — FR-017's "fetched by range, not held whole" is defeated by
the range the client asks for. Behind a ticked task and a green suite: every range served is
*correct*, so nothing that checks content can see this.
**Severity**: High. Scrolling back ~400 lines in an ordinary terminal empties the pane and leaves it
empty for seconds; scrolling faster keeps it empty for the whole gesture and then trips the
liveness keepalive (see [BUG-022](BUG-022.md), which this one triggers).
**Feature**: `010-daemon-session-persistence` (FR-016/FR-017/FR-018, quickstart S10). Observed on
branch `docs/cleanup-completed-features`, client+daemon `0.10.0` built 2026-08-25
**Reported**: 2026-08-25
**Status**: **Fixed** (2026-08-27)

## Resolution (2026-08-27)

Both halves of the cost were in the range the client asked for, and both are now decided by one
function, `GridCache::needed_scrollback` (`crates/micold-client/src/grid.rs`). It sits there because
that is where the facts it needs already are — the viewport top, the trim watermark, the row count
and which lines are cached — and because it can then be tested without a window. What is left in
`scroll_view` is the correlation id and the socket.

**The range is sized by the viewport, not by the depth.** It starts at the first revealed line that
is neither cached nor already asked for; runs forward only to the end of that un-cached run, capped
at one viewport and at `viewport_top`; and extends one viewport *further into history*, which is the
direction the gesture is travelling. At most two viewports, at any depth. The forward cap is not
decoration: without it the second request re-fetches most of what the first one is already bringing.

**A range already in flight is a range this view has coming.** `App::scrollback_inflight` records
what has been asked for and not yet answered, and `needed_scrollback` treats those lines exactly as
it treats cached ones. This is what makes the prefetch pay. During a fast gesture nothing has
arrived yet, so without the record every notch re-computes the same un-cached run and re-sends it —
which is the storm, not the range size. The record is released by the matching `ScrollbackResponse`
(*before* the lines are applied, so a range the daemon could not serve stops suppressing its own
re-request), dropped wholesale on disconnect (`req`s are per-connection; a range awaiting an answer
that will never come would suppress the very request that would fill those rows), and dropped with
the session's grid.

**Measured over the same gesture**, by the end-to-end gate below — 150 wheel notches of two lines,
driven through the real dispatcher against a real `Outbox`:

| | requests | lines asked for | lines revealed |
|---|---|---|---|
| the rule as reported (probe G) | 150 | 33,000 | 300 |
| now | 5 | 351 | 300 |

The 33,000 is not an estimate: probe G reverts both halves at once and prints what the same gesture
puts on the wire. It is also exactly `sum(2, 4, … 300) + 150 x 69` — the depth-sized range plus the
prefetch that was still running under it — which is the quadratic the report described, arithmetic
rather than adjective.

**The daemon is unchanged.** It served every one of those ranges correctly; correct and enormous is
what a request sized by scroll depth is, and no amount of work on the serving side makes 33,000
lines the right answer to a 300-line gesture. `protocol.md` §6 now states the client's side of that
as a requirement, since nothing in the message shape prevented it.

**Gates** — six on the range (`crates/micold-client/src/grid.rs`), three on the gesture
(`crates/micold-client/src/main.rs`):

| Gate | Asserts |
|---|---|
| `a_scrolled_view_asks_for_the_rows_it_reveals_not_the_history_beneath_them` | the reported defect: 600 lines back does not reach the live tail, and asks for at most two viewports |
| `the_size_of_a_request_does_not_grow_with_the_depth_of_the_scroll` | 100, 600 and 3000 lines back are the same size |
| `a_range_already_asked_for_is_not_asked_for_again` | an in-flight range suppresses its own re-request, and the next few notches with it |
| `the_next_request_starts_where_the_last_one_stops` | and does not overlap it |
| `a_view_whose_revealed_rows_are_cached_asks_for_nothing` | the cache is consulted at all |
| `a_request_never_reaches_below_the_oldest_retained_line` | the prefetch is clamped to the trim watermark |
| `a_scroll_gesture_asks_once_per_viewport_not_once_per_notch` | 150 real `TerminalScrolled` messages produce a handful of bounded requests, not one per notch |
| `a_range_whose_answer_brought_nothing_is_asked_for_again` | the in-flight record is released by its answer |
| `a_disconnect_releases_the_ranges_that_will_never_be_answered` | and by a dropped connection |

The last three drive the real dispatcher and read a real `Outbox`, because what the report measured
was a *gesture* — a hundred and fifty messages and what came out of them — and no assertion about
one request can see that.

**Probes** (2026-08-27). Each reverts one layer and runs the two targets that could notice as
**separate** `cargo test` invocations: one invocation stops at its first failing target, and the
binary's tests run before the library's, which in [BUG-023](BUG-023.md) silently hid three gates.

| Probe | Reverted | Fails at |
|---|---|---|
| A | the range runs to `viewport_top` — **the reported defect exactly** | `grid.rs:400`, `:423`, `:462`; `main.rs:1872` |
| B | no prefetch: the request stops at the revealed line | `grid.rs:444`; `main.rs:1862` |
| C | an in-flight range is not treated as coming | `grid.rs:440`, `:463`; `main.rs:1862` |
| D | the answer does not release its range | **nothing — see below**; after the gate was added, `main.rs:1923` |
| E | a disconnect leaves the ranges recorded | `main.rs:1948` |
| F | the prefetch is not clamped to the trim watermark | `grid.rs:493` |
| G | A and C together — measurement, not a gate (the figures above) | — |
| H | the cache is not consulted, only the in-flight record | `grid.rs:478` |

**Probe D is the one worth reading.** It deleted the line that releases an in-flight range when its
answer arrives, and the whole suite passed. Every other gate stops at the request: none of them let
an answer come back and then scrolled again, so nothing said the record was ever cleared — and a
record that is never cleared is a range this view will never ask for twice. That is invisible while
the answer carries the lines, because the cache then holds them and the two conditions agree; it is
permanent when it does not, because the daemon trimmed them or the session went away, and those rows
stay blank for as long as the window stays there. A storm traded for a hole.
`a_range_whose_answer_brought_nothing_is_asked_for_again` was written in response, and only then did
the probe fail. This is the second feature running in which a probe found a layer nothing was
testing; the lesson is the same one BUG-022 recorded and it keeps being worth the twenty minutes.

**What is deliberately not done: the blank rows themselves.** The report's third suggestion — keep
the last painted rows on screen while a range is in flight, so a slow fetch degrades into a stale
view rather than an empty one — is not implemented. Blanking is now brief because the fetch is
bounded, and painting a row with content that is not that row's is a different and worse failure
than painting it empty. Anyone who wants it should treat it as its own change with its own gates,
not as part of this one.

**Not re-measured on a display.** The reproduction that produced this report — Xvfb `:83`, a Regular
terminal holding ~4,010 lines, 50 notches per 3 s — has not been re-run since the fix. The gates
drive the same dispatcher the window does and the figures above come from that dispatcher, but
nobody has watched a real pane stay filled during a real gesture, and the CPU columns in the report
have no "after" row. The daemon-side figures in particular are inferred from the reduction in what
is asked of it, not observed.

**[BUG-022](BUG-022.md), which this report names as a downstream consequence, was fixed separately**
on the same day and does not depend on this one. That is the right split: this fix makes the
keepalive far less likely to expire during a scroll, and BUG-022 makes a window survive it when
something else does.

## Description

Quickstart S10, run headlessly on Xvfb `:83` (lavapipe) against a Regular terminal holding ~4,010
lines, `scrollback_lines: 10000` so the whole history is retained.

**Expected** (S10): *"scrolling stays responsive (history is fetched by range, not held whole)"*.

**Observed**: the fetched range is not the revealed rows — it is everything from the revealed line
to the live tail, re-requested on every wheel notch.

At a **brisk human speed** (50 notches per 3 s, ≈17 notches/s, a 1 s pause between rounds; one notch
= 2 lines):

| round | notches so far | lines back | top line shown | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50 | 100 | `3836` | fine |
| 2 | 100 | 200 | `3736` | fine |
| 3 | 150 | 300 | `3636` | fine |
| 4 | 200 | 400 | `3536` | fine |
| 5 | 250 | 500 | — | **pane entirely blank** |
| 6 | 300 | 600 | — | **pane entirely blank** |

Stop touching it and the content arrives ~5 s later, at exactly the right place (`3336` at the top —
600 lines back, as asked). So the *data* is right and the *latency* is the defect. Every step above
is exactly 100 lines per 50 notches, which is also this scenario's continuity check passing.

At **fast speed** (300 notches in 3 s) the pane stays blank and the daemon keeps working long after
the input stops. CPU (ticks of 10 ms, `/proc/<pid>/stat` utime+stime):

| window | client | daemon |
|---|---|---|
| during the 3 s of scrolling | 29.0 s | 2.1 s |
| the next 5 s (no input) | 1.5 s | **5.2 s** |
| the following 10 s (no input) | 3.1 s | **7.3 s** |
| the following 15 s (no input) | 4.7 s | 0.1 s |

The daemon is saturated for ~15 s of *silence* after a 3 s gesture. (The client's own 29 s is
lavapipe software rendering and says nothing about a real GPU; the daemon's does not render.)

## Root cause

`crates/micold-client/src/main.rs:836-855`, `scroll_view`:

```rust
let top = (vt - new_off as i64).max(oldest);
// ... find `need_from` = the lowest un-cached line in [top, top + rows)
d.send(ClientMsg::ScrollbackRequest {
    session: id,
    req,
    ranges: vec![LineId(from)..LineId(vt)],   // <-- to the live tail, not `from + rows`
});
```

`vt` is the viewport top of the *live* grid. The viewport is ~69 rows, but the request runs from the
first un-cached revealed line all the way to the tail, so its size grows with scroll depth: 400
lines back asks for 400 lines, 600 back asks for 600 — once per notch. Total work over a gesture is
quadratic in depth.

The daemon serves each of them faithfully and serially, under the session's shared terminal lock,
rebuilding a style/hyperlink palette per line (`crates/micold-daemon/src/server.rs:473-510`,
`crates/micold-daemon/src/framer.rs:197-240`). Nothing coalesces or supersedes the queued requests,
so a 300-notch gesture leaves ~300 overlapping ranges to grind through.

`scroll_view`'s own doc comment states the visible consequence as if it were the design — *"un-fetched
lines render blank until the `ScrollbackResponse` fills them (FR-016/017)"*. Blanking briefly while a
*viewport-sized* range is in flight is defensible; blanking for seconds because the request was
depth-sized is not, and it is what FR-017's "not held whole" was written to prevent.

## Reproduction

1. Open a Regular terminal session, `seq 1 4000 | sed 's/$/ ....../'`.
2. Scroll up with the wheel, ~17 notches/s, for four seconds.
3. The pane empties. Stop, wait ~5 s: it fills in at the correct position.

## Suggested fix

Request `LineId(from)..LineId(from + rows)` (plus whatever prefetch margin is wanted) rather than
`..vt`, and drop or supersede an outstanding request when a newer scroll position makes it
irrelevant — the correlation id (`req`) is already on the wire for exactly that. Keeping the last
painted rows on screen while a range is in flight, instead of painting blank, would also make a slow
fetch degrade into a stale view rather than an empty one.

## What passed in the same run

The rest of S10 holds and is not in question: continuity across a fully detached interval (200,000
lines produced with no client attached, then 4,000 more, all present and exactly ordered on
reattach), the scrollbar sized from the advertised watermark (24 px thumb for 69 visible rows of
~4,015 — 1.9% against an expected 1.7%), clamping at the oldest retained line (repeated 400-notch
over-scroll at `scrollback_lines: 170` pins to the oldest line and stays there, no error), and
selection anchored to line ids under new output (a selection of lines 3968–3971 stayed on those
lines as five new lines pushed the view up).
