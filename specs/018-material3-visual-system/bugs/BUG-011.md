# Bug Report: two §1.3 pairs clear AA at rest and fail it under a press — the snackbar's action and the untyped filter chip, both dark-scheme only

**Type**: Untested flow. Both pairs are **inside** §1.3's enumeration and both clear AA at rest, so
every gate that existed before FR-004b was right about them. FR-004b changed the measurement — a
contrast obligation is now measured with the heaviest state layer the element can carry composited —
and these two are what the new measurement condemns. Unlike BUG-010 this is not drift: nothing is
drawn on a host the contract forbids. The contract's own rule now forbids something the contract
also permits, which is a conflict FR-004b has to resolve.

**Severity**: Low — both are dark-scheme only, both miss by under 3%, and both misses are *transient*
(the pressed layer, which is on screen while a finger is down). Neither is a legibility failure of
BUG-009's kind. It is a conformance gap, and one that only became visible because the gate that
would have shown it was written this week.

**Feature**: `018-material3-visual-system` (§1.3 the contrast obligation table, §5 state layers,
§7.6 chips, §7.8 the snackbar; FR-004b, SC-008h).

**Reported**: 2026-08-25

**Status**: Open

## Description

Found by T160 and T165 — BUG-010's own composited assertion and the neutral-host sweep it required —
not by anyone looking at a screen. `crates/micold-core/tests/tokens_contrast.rs` now measures every
§1.3 pair twice, at rest and with the heaviest layer the element drawing it can carry. Two rows fail
the second measurement, both in the dark scheme:

| Pair | Element | Layer | At rest | Composited | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `inverse_primary` / `inverse_surface` | the snackbar's `Dismiss`, a text button | `inverse_primary` @ 10% (`pressed`) | 4.99:1 | **4.37:1** | 4.5 |
| `on_surface_variant` / `surface_variant` | the sidebar's **Untyped** filter chip, active | `on_surface_variant` @ 10% (`pressed`) | 5.48:1 | **4.47:1** | 4.5 |

Both are pinned in `UNDER_AA_COMPOSITED` in that file. The pin is not an exemption: the assertion
fails if the set gains a member **and** if it loses one, so fixing either of these breaks the build
until its row is deleted, and a drift in either measurement breaks it as soon as the figure moves by
more than a hundredth. That shape is deliberate — BUG-010 existed because its predecessor recorded an
excluded class in a module doc, and a doc comment reports nothing when the exclusion stops being
true.

The light scheme passes both. That asymmetry is not luck: in the light scheme the layer moves the
background *away* from the foreground on these two pairs, and in the dark scheme it moves it toward.
A rule that reads only one scheme would have found neither.

### The figure is 4.37, not the 4.34 or 4.40 a hand calculation gives

Worth recording, because it cost a round. §5's opacities are `f32`. `state::PRESSED as f64` is
0.100000001…, not 0.1, and on this pair one channel lands exactly on a rounding boundary — 8.0 +
202.5 rounds to 211 at a true 0.1 and to 210 at the `f32` value, which is the whole difference
between 4.40 and 4.37. The measurement has to read §5's constant rather than restate it, which is
FR-029a's argument arriving somewhere nobody expected it.

## Root Cause Analysis

Two roles were given tones that clear AA with about 0.5:1 of headroom, and a state layer eats 0.5:1.

`inverse_primary` is Material's own baseline role, primary tone 80 in the light scheme and tone 40 in
the dark, against `inverse_surface` at neutral 20 / neutral 90. That pairing yields 4.99:1 by
construction — it is *designed* to sit just above AA, because the point of `inverse_primary` is to be
the lightest accent that still reads on an inverted surface. There is no headroom left for a layer,
and there was never meant to be: Material measures the pair at rest, and §5's layers are Material's
too. FR-004b is stricter than the system it is applied to. That is a defensible thing to be — WCAG
measures rendered pixels and grants no exemption for a state that only lasts while a finger is down —
but it means the conflict is structural, not a slip.

The chip is the same shape with a different cause: `on_surface_variant` on `surface_variant` is a
deliberately *low*-contrast pair, the muted one, and 5.48:1 in the dark scheme is the least headroom
in the whole neutral table.

Neither was catchable before this week. §1.3 proved pairs, §5 proved opacities, and nothing multiplied
them — which is the sentence BUG-010's report already contains, and this is that sentence's remaining
half.

## Recommended Fix

**Decide the rule before touching either colour** — as BUG-010 did, and for the same reason: there are
three candidate answers and they differ in what they cost, not in whether they work.

1. **Narrow the host where a host exists, and give FR-004b a clause for where one does not.** The chip
   takes a permitted neutral fill — `surface_container_high` with `on_surface`, exactly the move the
   `Info` banner just made — which costs the untyped chip its muted look and makes it read closer to
   a typed one. The snackbar cannot be narrowed, so FR-004b gains an explicit, *measured* exception
   with a stated ceiling rather than a silent one, and the pin in `tokens_contrast.rs` becomes its
   executable form.
2. **Change the snackbar action's role to `inverse_on_surface`** (10.17:1 at rest, 8.54:1 pressed —
   passes with room). It costs the action its accent: `Dismiss` becomes the same colour as the message
   beside it, and `style.rs`'s own comment argues that the accent *is* the role's purpose. This is the
   only option that closes the bug outright with no exception, and the most expensive in affordance.
3. **Exempt the pressed layer from the obligation**, on the grounds that it is transient and
   user-initiated. Cheapest, and the one to be most suspicious of: it is the "state layers on neutral
   hosts are exempt" argument BUG-010 already measured and rejected, returning in a narrower costume.
   It also weakens FR-004b four days after it was written, which is a high bar to clear on two
   marginal findings.

Then:

1. `/speckit.bugfix.patch` — amend 018's spec/contract with the rule chosen above.
2. `/speckit.bugfix.verify` — confirm consistency across spec, plan and tasks.
3. `/speckit.implement` — apply it, and delete the corresponding row from `UNDER_AA_COMPOSITED`;
   the test fails until that row goes, which is how a fixed pin announces itself.
