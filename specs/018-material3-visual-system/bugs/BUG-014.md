# Bug Report: a press draws the ripple over the surface's own hover or pressed layer — every click stacks two layers for half a second

**Type**: Spec conflict. FR-024c says the ripple is drawn "at the pressed opacity, so it **composes
with** the existing state layers rather than replacing them"; §5 and FR-022a (BUG-013) say a control
draws "the heaviest of those state layers, **not their sum**", and FR-004b's contrast measurement rests
on that. The code does what FR-024c says, and so breaks what §5 says.

**Severity**: Low. It is transient — the ripple expands for 300 ms and fades over 200 ms whether or not
the pointer is still held, so no steady state stacks — but it is on **every** mouse press of every
rippling surface, in both schemes, which makes it the most frequently drawn state-layer composite in the
application after hover itself.

**Feature**: `018-material3-visual-system` (§5, §5.1; FR-004b, FR-022a, FR-024a, FR-024c).

**Reported**: 2026-09-14

**Status**: Fixed

**Patched**: 2026-09-14

## Description

Found while recording BUG-013's rendered-frame pass (tasks.md T179 notes that every sample was taken
after the ripple had faded, and why). Traced in code rather than sampled on a frame:

- `material::Ripple::draw` (`crates/micold-client/src/ui/material/ripple.rs`) draws the child first,
  then a circle at `state::PRESSED × strength` over it.
- The child is a button (or chip, menu entry, tree row, picker entry, icon button) whose style already
  draws its own layer for iced's `Status`: `HOVER` (0.08) while the pointer is over it, `PRESSED`
  (0.10) while it is held.
- The ripple's strength is 1.0 for the whole expansion and the circle covers the element once it has
  expanded (radius = distance to the furthest corner), so at t = 300 ms the entire element carries both.

| Press | Child's layer | Ripple at full strength | Composite at t = 300 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| held | pressed 0.10 | 0.10 | **0.19** — `1 − 0.9 × 0.9` |
| clicked and released (the common case) | hover 0.08 | 0.10 | **0.172** — `1 − 0.92 × 0.9` |

Those are exactly the two composites BUG-013 measured through its probe of `tokens_contrast.rs`: at
0.172 the light scheme's `on_primary/primary` composites to 4.35:1, at 0.19 to 4.03:1, both under AA,
and the outlined button's border on `surface_container_high` falls under 3:1. For ~200 ms of each
press, then recovering as the ripple fades.

The focus indicator is not involved: `TakesTheKeyboard` wraps the ripple, and after BUG-013 a pointer
press draws no indicator.

## Root Cause Analysis

Two requirements written for different things. FR-024c was written about the ripple's *colour* — it
must be the state-layer colour so it reads as a state layer — and "composes with" was a way of saying it
does not *replace* the hover/press styling. §5's mutual exclusion was written about the state table.
Neither considered the pixel where both land. The wrapper also cannot see its child's status (iced's
`button::Status` is chosen inside the button's own `update`), which is the same structural reason
BUG-013's wrapper stacked its layer.

There is a real tension in any remedy: the ripple is only visible as a *difference* against what is
under it, and under it is already 0.08–0.10 of the same colour. A ripple that "tops up" to 0.10 over a
hovered surface differs from its surroundings by about 2% and cannot be seen, which would break FR-024a.

## Recommended Fix

**Decide the rule first.** Candidates:

1. **The ripple replaces the surface's layer while it runs**: while a ripple is animating, the wrapped
   surface draws its resting fill, the circle draws at the pressed opacity, and as it fades it settles
   toward the layer the surface is about to resume (hover if the pointer is still over it, pressed if
   still held, none otherwise) before handing back. Conforms to §5 and keeps the ripple visible. Costs
   a way for the wrapper to suppress the child's layer at draw time, touching every rippling style.
2. **Make the ripple exempt as motion**: amend §5 and FR-022a to steady states, keep FR-024c's
   composition, and pin with a test that the stacked composite exists only while a ripple animates and
   is gone within `medium_2 + short_4`. Material's own implementations sum hover and ripple the same
   way. No visual change.

**Decision** (2026-09-14): candidate 1 — the ripple replaces the surface's layer while it runs
(FR-024h, SC-008j). The accepted visible change is that a hovered button shows its resting fill outside
the circle while it grows. The yield is draw-scoped in `style::state_layer`/`state_fill`, the two
helpers every rippling style already composes with, so no style had to change; the ripple reads back
the heaviest layer they were asked for and fades to it. FR-024c's "composes with" is struck.

Then `/speckit.bugfix.patch`, `/speckit.bugfix.verify`, and `/speckit.implement` with the failing test
first.
